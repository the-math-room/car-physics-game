HORSEPOWER SLIDER PLAYTEST

The current game keeps an $8,000 parts budget and offers 120–300 hp in 1 hp
increments. Open Modify engine to preview horsepower, price, added hardware
mass, total-build power-to-weight, torque and power curves, and marginal cost
and mass for the next 10 hp. The engine does not change until installed.

Model assumptions
This is a smooth family of fictional engine packages, not a universal law or
measured dyno dataset. All power is crankshaft mechanical horsepower (1 hp =
0.745699872 kW); the drivetrain then applies 88% efficiency. Peak horsepower is
normalized analytically on the piecewise-linear torque curves, including peaks
between RPM knots. More output shifts the torque distribution upward in RPM,
but torque drops strongly near the 7,000 RPM limiter. All engines share that
limiter; their best shift thresholds need not be the same.

For h = horsepower minus 120:
  price = 25h + 0.10h² dollars, rounded to $10;
  added hardware mass = 0.15h + 0.002h² kg, rounded to 0.1 kg.
Prices are incremental classroom package allowances (workshop labor supplied).
The increasing price and mass represent supporting hardware, cooling and
strengthening in this fictional family. They are not new retail quotations.
The old price-anchor research remains in balance-report.txt. No new claims
about a specific commercial engine or kit are made.

Playtesting
Run npm run analyze to regenerate engine-slider-results.json. The exhaustive
sweep tries all 181 horsepower settings, all other catalog combinations, and
nine shift thresholds from 5,000 to 7,000 RPM in 250 RPM increments. Over-budget
builds are skipped. This is a clean-finish physics comparison; steering affects
whether the player finishes, not the clean-run longitudinal timing.

The best sampled budget build uses 279 hp, drag tires and a short-throw shifter.
It uses the full $8,000. The next horsepower increment crosses a package budget
boundary: the buyer must sacrifice something else. The 300 hp engine costs
$7,740 and adds 91.8 kg; it cannot afford a traction or shift upgrade. This is
why maximum horsepower is not the fastest complete build. We do NOT claim
that mass alone makes the stronger engine slower with identical equipment.

A 269 hp engine with drag tires, short-throw shifter and undertray is within
roughly 0.12 s of the best. A 260 hp build with tall gearing is within about
0.18 s. Small optional parts remain smaller influences than power and grip;
their physical effects were not inflated for balance.
